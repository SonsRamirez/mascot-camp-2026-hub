Day 1, in detail

The Hersheypark playbook

This is the big, one-of-a-kind event — Hersheypark hosting 100+ mascots for a parade and group photo. Everything below is from Hersheypark's official details.

Arrival & parking

  • Follow the blue arrows in from Hersheypark Drive; stop at the Parking Toll Plaza to scan your pre-purchased parking pass.
  • Park in GATESIDE — Lot N: the dedicated pre-paid lot right beside Hersheypark Arena, between the Arena and Hersheypark Stadium (lower-right on the map below).
  • Bring the suit around the left side of the Arena, to the back — that's where you suit up (marked "Changing Rooms").
  • Golf carts are available to help carry costumes down.
  • Address: 100 Hersheypark Drive, Hershey, PA 17033.

Suiting up & performing

  • Come dressed in the clothes you wear under the suit — costumes go on inside the Arena (closed to the public).
  • Wear performing undergarments under your clothes at check-in to save time.
  • Performers are in costume ~10–11 AM (parade + photo). A rest period follows before any later meet-and-greet window.
  • Need a private space to change? Tell Brewer in advance.

After the performance

  • Put the costume back in the car, then re-enter the park from the main gate with your wristband.
  • Mascot campers do NOT do meet-and-greets — the afternoon is for rides + the water park.
  • Meet-and-greets (adults 18+ only) run until 5 PM latest.
  • Then: the Campers-vs-Staff Challenge, and off to Millersville.

Beat the heat 🥵

  • Camp emphasizes healthy performing — Wyatt can sit out any time he needs to.
  • Bring cooling gear: cooling towel, personal fan, ice packs, sweatband.
  • Hydrate hard; refillable water bottle is a must.
  • Even at Sunday's game there are lower-strenuous options if it's hot.

🎥 Filming & social media at Hersheypark

You're welcome to capture Wyatt during the event — but while the park is open to the public, a few rules apply:

  • Mascots are not allowed on rides, and no filming/photographing on rides.
  • No drones or GoPros; no drone filming over the complex.
  • No interviewing guests or staff without prior approval.
  • No live streaming anywhere in Hersheypark, The Boardwalk water park, ZooAmerica, or the resorts.
  • Bringing a detachable-lens camera? Tell Amanda in advance so she can alert security.

Local media may be invited to cover the parade, and Hershey will capture its own b-roll. Any concern about Wyatt appearing on camera? Tell Amanda (see Contacts).

Tagging: tag @Hersheypark (keep "Hersheypark" one word) with #Hersheypark and #HersheyparkHappy.

Heads-up: Hershey's media policy does not apply at Millersville — Keystone asks each family to say how they want their mascot (and themselves) filmed or not. See Status.

Good to know

Youth protection & conduct

The two-adult rule

Keystone uses the same accountability rule as Scouting: any time a youth contacts an adult, a second adult is copied. If Wyatt needs to contact Erin or a trainer, he copies Dom (parent/guardian). Adults contacting a youth copy Erin. That's the entire "youth protection training" — there's no separate online course.

Dom's PA volunteer clearances (State Police / Child Abuse / FBI) are complete and already on file with Keystone, satisfying the adult-clearance requirement.

For the curious

About the camp

Keystone Mascots has run mascot training camps since 1990 at Millersville University, billed as the most diverse gathering of experienced mascot trainers anywhere. Over four days Wyatt will work on character development, acting skills, safety, costume care, crowd interaction, and sportsmanship, plus PIAA/NCAA regulations and liability basics. He'll build skits for games and pep rallies, get one-on-one counseling, take home video footage of his practices and performance, and a camp manual to keep learning after camp ends.

The camp has been featured by USA Today, the Washington Post, ESPN Magazine, ABC, NBC and more. It wraps with a graduation performance at a Lancaster Stormers game — a real crowd, a real finale. keystonemascots.com ↗
